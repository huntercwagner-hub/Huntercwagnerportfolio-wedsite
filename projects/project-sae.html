Project Overview

The SAE Aero Design competition challenges university teams to design and build a radio-controlled electric aircraft capable of carrying a liquid payload while meeting strict design, performance, and takeoff/landing requirements. For the 2024 season, UWF Argonautics competed in the Micro Class, developing an aircraft designed for maximum efficiency, structural stability, and aerodynamic performance under a 450W power limitation.

My Contributions

  • Created detailed CAD models of the fuselage and payload system using SolidWorks.
  • Assisted in the Finite Element Analysis (FEA) to validate strength and stiffness under flight loads.
  • Participated in the manufacturing process — converting CAD designs to 2D DXF files and using the UWF laser cutter for precision part fabrication.
  • Helped integrate the propulsion and control systems within the fuselage while maintaining optimal center of gravity alignment.
  • Supported the framework subteam in assembling and ultracoating the final aircraft to ensure aerodynamic efficiency and durability.

Design & Analysis Highlights

The aircraft design followed a high-wing monoplane configuration for increased stability and lift efficiency. The fuselage used a semi-monocoque structure made of birch and lauan hardwood, balancing strength, weight, and cost-effectiveness. The payload container was cylindrical with internal baffles to minimize liquid slosh and maintain the center of gravity during flight.

Key analyses included:

  • Scoring Optimization: Reduced wingspan and empty weight to maximize SAE flight score.
  • Propulsion Selection: Cobra 3515/14 950Kv motor, chosen to align with the 450W limiter while providing sufficient thrust (5.79 lbf static).
  • Flight Simulation: Performed with XFLR5 to determine coefficients of lift, drag, and center of pressure stability across a range of attack angles.
  • Control Surface Sizing: Optimized aileron, elevator, and rudder areas for improved stability and maneuverability.

Testing & Manufacturing

The landing gear system utilized a tricycle layout to enhance taxiing control and reduce the risk of crash landings. Each gear assembly was designed to withstand loads exceeding three times the aircraft’s weight during touchdown. The liquid payload container design was refined to drain 67 fluid ounces in under one minute through a 1/8-inch outlet hole.

All structural components were laser cut from hardwood sheets and assembled with cyanoacrylate adhesive. After integration of the propulsion system, control surfaces, and wiring, the aircraft was ultracoated for aerodynamic smoothness and protection.

I also learned how to manage technical risk, balance design trade-offs under constraints, and collaborate effectively within a multidisciplinary engineering team.

Through my hands-on involvement, I developed a deeper understanding of how mechanical design principles influence aircraft performance, from structural rigidity to stability under changing payload conditions. This project strengthened my skills in engineering design communication, project coordination, and problem-solving under real-world competition pressure.
